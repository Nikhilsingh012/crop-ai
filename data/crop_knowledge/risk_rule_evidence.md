# Crop risk source audit and candidate rules

Reviewed 2026-10-22. This report separates evidence for a biological/weather relationship from evidence that supports the exact calculation currently performed by CropAI. An executable forecast rule needs both: source-supported weather conditions and a defined/applicable evaluation period. The current engine's hourly threshold evaluator normally assesses 48 forecast hours; its index is an exposure share, not damage probability.

## Newly executable rule: IMD KALP wheat grain-filling heat indicator

**Source:** IMD, [KALP Operational Manual](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/agromet/kalp/kalp.pdf), undated, Forecast / Likely Impact table, printed p. 9. The source's exact crop-weather trigger is: “Tmax > 30°C during grain filling stage of the wheat crop.” The table describes shortened grain filling, shriveled grains and yield loss as likely impact. The manual describes a five-day crop-specific forecast workflow (Executive Summary, printed p. 6; system overview, p. 9; forecast display, p. 11).

**Code mapping:** `wheat_grain_filling_daily_tmax_above_30c_imd_kalp` applies to wheat, grain filling, India; `temperature_max` from daily `temperature_2m` forecasts, °C, strict `>30`; five consecutive forecast dates, each with a reported maximum. A day at exactly 30°C does not trigger. A trigger reports the date and forecast maximum as a deterministic exposure indicator. It has no risk index, severity, alert probability, or crop-loss probability. The evaluator requires a complete five-day daily forecast, so missing Tmax/date or a date gap is unavailable rather than treated as zero. The daily weather feed already supplies maximum temperature in °C; the farm `/weather/risk` path now passes the source's five-day window internally while preserving its response shape. The nine prior heat candidates remain provisional and untouched.

**Evidence category and limitation:** IMD's KALP example is an operational agrometeorological advisory threshold, not a published calibrated probability model or a yield-loss function. The local crop response and 2 m gridded Tmax proxy may need validation. The indicator only states that the source threshold appears in the forecast. It does not state that damage is certain or quantify damage.

The western-UP and Punjab JHULSACAST leads remain unavailable. The complete western-UP paper and conflicting definitions are documented in [the JHULSACAST audit](jhulsacast_source_audit.md). The Punjab publication page exposes only its abstract; its four-page full paper could not be independently accessed to verify the model details. Neither model is executable from the verified material available here.

### Forecast horizon and late-blight candidate

Open-Meteo's official [Forecast API documentation](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) says forecasts default to seven days and allow up to 16 (`forecast_days`); `forecast_hours` controls hourly-series length. The documentation lists hourly 2 m temperature, relative humidity, precipitation, total cloud cover and low cloud cover (fog up to 3 km), while warning that model coverage, resolution and variable availability vary by model/location. CropAI currently requests `forecast_hours=49`; `_browser_forecast` accepts at most 200 hourly entries, `/weather/risk` keeps only 48 future hours, and the engine evaluates only the first 48.

The best current multi-day candidate is PAU's [potato late-blight guidance](https://pau.edu/potato/favourable_cond.php), which states: “When winter rains occur raising the humidity to > 90% and cloudy or foggy days persist for 6-7 days with atmospheric temperature in the range of 10-20°C (during November-December), late blight develops rapidly...” The page also says first appearance in Punjab is generally in November when the main-season crop is about 40–50 days old, and notes early September-planted crops generally escape. This supports disease-favourable weather and disease spread, not confirmed infection or calibrated damage probability.

**Do not implement this rule yet.** A seven-day horizon (168 future hours; 169 API slots including the current hour) could cover the stated period, and Open-Meteo exposes several useful variables. PAU describes persistent *days* and a conjunction of winter rain, RH, cloud/fog, temperature and crop/calendar context; it does not define hourly thresholds, a daily aggregation algorithm, or how many qualifying hours make one qualifying day. CropAI also lacks planting date/crop age, crop season, inoculum/disease observations, and an official late-blight advisory feed. Adding variables and choosing hourly-to-daily cutoffs would invent parts of the rule. The smallest defensible next step is PAU/ICAR clarification or a source publishing an explicit daily forecast criterion, plus planting-date/season context. The output must remain a favourable-weather indicator, never confirmed disease.

The existing weather UI and hazard cards render null scores as `UNAVAILABLE` and show an availability explanation. No frontend change is needed to keep unavailable assessments distinct from low risk.

## Follow-up implementation: ICAR-CPRI potato tuberization night-temperature condition

**New executable rule:** `potato_tuberization_daily_tmin_at_or_above_20c_icar_cpri`.

**Source and exact passage:** [ICAR-CPRI, “Potato at a Glance,” Indian Horticulture (Nov–Dec 2019)](https://www.icar.gov.in/sites/default/files/2022-06/IH-November-December-2019.pdf), climate and soil requirement: “Optimum temperature: 18–22°C (< 20 °C night temp. for tuberization.” The sheet identifies the crop as *Solanum tuberosum* and lists Indian potato production zones. This supports potato, tuberization, Celsius and a nighttime upper condition below 20°C. It does not quantify crop damage, define a damaging duration, or provide a probability model.

**Evidence-to-code mapping:** The assessment uses the next two consecutive daily forecasts and flags a daily minimum (Tmin) at or above 20°C as outside the source’s stated preferred tuberization condition. Tmin is the provider’s daily proxy for night temperature. The two consecutive future calendar days are the application forecast horizon, not a source-defined damage duration. Missing Tmin observations or non-consecutive forecast dates make the assessment unavailable. Geographic applicability is limited to the Indian crop catalog context; the source itself is a national ICAR-CPRI guide. Results carry the source and limitations, have no index/severity/probability, and are not described as confirmed damage.

**Other researched candidates that remain unavailable:**

- The IMD [KALP manual](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/agromet/kalp/kalp.pdf), p. 10, lists wheat yellow-rust weather criteria `Tmax >20°C`, `Tmin 10–15°C`, and `RH >85%`; p. 19 discusses a wheat grain-filling example and says an alarm is raised when two or more forecast parameters match. CropAI passes five daily Tmax/Tmin forecasts but only 48 future hourly RH samples; it has no five-day daily RHmax forecast field. It therefore cannot assess the complete five-day condition. The exact RH aggregation and two-parameter counting semantics also need confirmation before an executable rule can be claimed. This remains a weather-favourability lead, never confirmed disease.
- The same IMD manual, p. 17, gives wheat grain filling an “optimum minimum temperature requirement of 10°C” but does not state which side of that threshold should trigger risk. No directional rule was inferred.
- ICAR-CPRI’s same potato fact sheet gives a crop condition rather than a forecast damage response; the new potato output is deliberately a condition indicator only. The former provisional potato heat record remains provisional and excluded.

The nine older provisional heat records remain excluded from executable scoring. JHULSACAST remains unavailable as documented in [the source audit](jhulsacast_source_audit.md).

## New executable rule: rice flowering hourly temperature exposure

**Rule ID:** `rice_flowering_hourly_temperature_above_35c_icar_review`.

**Original source and passage:** Kingra, Kaur & Kaur, *Climate change impacts on rice (Oryza sativa) productivity and strategies for its sustainable management*, Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences 89(2), 171–180 (2019), [full ICAR ePubs PDF](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/86981/35562/222711). Printed p. 4 / journal p. 173 says: “development of florets is extremely sensitive to temperature higher than 35°C.” Printed p. 7 says: “Anthesis was observed as the most sensitive stage to high temperature” and “Heading and flowering appeared to be the most sensitive stages of rice affected by high temperature.”

**Evidence-to-code mapping:** rice (*Oryza sativa L.*); flowering/anthesis; India; Open-Meteo hourly 2 m temperature in °C; strict `>35°C`; next 48 complete future hourly samples. The evaluator reports each matching timestamp and measured temperature, including exact-boundary behavior (35°C does not trigger). The paper does not prescribe a damaging duration: 48 hours is explicitly only the application's forecast display/evaluation window. An incomplete window is unavailable; it is not treated as no trigger. There is no index, severity, probability, crop-loss claim, action, or disease claim.

**Limitations:** the passage describes floret sensitivity but does not establish injury from a single forecast hour. Hourly 2 m air temperature is not panicle/floret temperature; exact anthesis hour, cultivar/ecotype and water status are not captured. The source is an Indian-authored review summarizing prior work; local performance may need validation. The assessment communicates forecast exposure only.

## Follow-up candidate decisions

- **Excess rainfall/waterlogging — deferred.** The ICAR-hosted study [Relative tolerance of crops to surface water stagnation](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JAE/article/view/14254), abstract, says stagnation “must be removed from the crop land within one day for sunflower, wheat and mustard,” two days for sorghum, pearl millet, pigeon pea and berseem seed, and 3–4 days for barley. It measures standing water and crop response, not a rain amount that forecasts standing water. CropAI has no field water-depth observation, soil infiltration/drainage model, or flood extent. Rainfall alone cannot execute this rule.
- **Cold/frost — deferred.** The inspected ICAR Manipur frost report is an outcome narrative, not a numeric crop/stage air-temperature trigger. Daily Tmin alone is insufficient to label frost injury without a supported temperature threshold, duration, and crop-stage mapping.
- **Dry spell/drought — deferred.** The inspected ICAR report describes a 38-day observed moth-bean dry spell but does not supply a forecast rainfall cut-off or soil-water state. Seven-day rain forecast is not an antecedent drought measurement.
- **Disease-favourable weather — deferred as a distinct hazard.** IMD KALP's manual (pp. 9–10; [official PDF](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/agromet/kalp/kalp.pdf)) gives yellow-rust wheat values Tmax >20°C, Tmin 10–15°C, RH >85%, while pp. 19–20 say an alarm is raised when two or more forecast parameter values match, and the example includes rainfall without specifying its threshold. The daily RH aggregation and parameter-counting/rainfall semantics are not fully defined. PAU's [potato late-blight guidance](https://pau.edu/potato/favourable_cond.php) requires winter rain, RH >90%, cloudy/foggy days, 10–20°C, and 6–7 persistent days; it does not define hourly-to-daily aggregation, and the saved crop context lacks planting date/crop age. Neither has been turned into a disease rule. The rice heat indicator remains in `HEAT_STRESS` and does not assess disease.
- **Other crop heat — deferred unless threshold plus measurement/duration align.** Candidate records remain provisional. The rice flowering rule above is a separately registered hourly condition indicator; it does not promote or change any of the nine prior provisional records.

## End-to-end hardening review (2026-10-22)

The rice evaluator now requires 48 distinct hourly timestamps exactly one hour apart, beginning at the first full hour after the forecast issue timestamp. It rejects duplicate or malformed timestamps, a gap in the window, missing or malformed/non-numeric/out-of-range temperature values, and a missing or malformed issue time. Comparisons remain strict `>35°C`; 35°C does not trigger. Timestamps displayed in the assessment retain the provider's original timestamp representation and are labelled with the forecast timezone (UTC only when the provider supplies no timezone). The evaluator does not synthesize values or report “no trigger” when the forecast is incomplete.

The wheat rule remains daily Tmax `>30°C` across five consecutive daily records at grain filling. The potato rule remains daily Tmin `>=20°C` on two consecutive future forecast dates at tuberization. Both still require complete inputs and preserve their deterministic, unscored output. India-scoped rules now require explicit India context through a supported state/UT or country; coordinates alone are not reverse-geocoded or treated as proof of country.

The saved-crop analysis route loads the user-owned database row, passes the row's crop name, growth stage, state/country, and coordinates through the risk service, and returns the risk hazards with that crop context. Route tests exercise this path using a realistic saved-crop row and a provider-shaped forecast, but do not connect to a running application, PostgreSQL instance, or live weather API.

## Rice at vegetative stage: rule-matching diagnosis

For a rice crop saved at **Vegetative**, the verified `rice_flowering_hourly_temperature_above_35c_icar_review` rule is correctly excluded because its source-backed stages are flowering/anthesis. The other rice heat candidate, `rice_flowering_heat_exposure_35c_india_2019`, remains provisional with incomplete evidence and is also flowering-scoped; it cannot be enabled for Vegetative. No verified rice vegetative rule is registered for heat, cold/frost, excess rainfall, dry spell, disease-favourable weather, or severe weather. Those hazards therefore remain unavailable; forecast rain or humidity alone does not establish those stresses.

A reviewed vegetative-stage moisture-stress lead is Patnaik et al. (2025), [ICAR Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/156250), abstract, lines 48–52: the TNAU Coimbatore field experiment imposed soil-moisture stress for 10, 15, 20 or 25 days at panicle initiation or flowering; the abstract identifies 20–25 days at panicle initiation as highly detrimental. This is not an executable forecast threshold: the experiment does not define a precipitation or air-temperature trigger, uses soil-moisture stress (not forecast rain), and applies to the panicle-initiation stage rather than the saved Vegetative label. CropAI lacks soil moisture and a 20–25-day forecast in this assessment. Do not translate this result into a rainless-day or rainfall-mm rule.

The risk response now includes a `skipped_rules` entry for each registered candidate excluded by evidence status, stage, region, or missing inputs. The farmer interface renders those reasons alongside the existing hazard status. This is diagnostic output; it does not make a provisional rule executable.

The crop catalog also resolves requested Hindi aliases to canonical crop identities (including Gehu, Gobi, Pyaz, Aloo, Karela, Kela and Mandua). Generic “peas” remains an explicit ambiguity between Field Pea and Garden Pea. Alias resolution does not imply that a crop has a verified hazard rule; for crops without such rules the assessment remains unavailable.

## Phase 3 saved-crop in-app alerts

Every trigger from an executable deterministic rule now produces at most one in-app WATCH alert per rule and forecast calendar date. The alert names the crop and rule, includes the expected local forecast date/time and timezone, states the measured condition and threshold, and carries the source URL. It is a weather indicator, not a confirmed disease or damage warning. Existing alerts are queried by saved crop and event title before insertion, so rerunning analysis does not create duplicates for the same forecast-date event. `Check saved crop alerts` runs current analysis for each saved crop; failures are reported separately and do not become low-risk assessments. No email, SMS or other external notification is sent.

## Crop-risk engine expansion review (2026-10-25)

The executable registry still contains three verified crop-specific weather rules, all in `HEAT_STRESS`: rice flowering/anthesis hourly temperature condition, wheat grain-filling daily Tmax condition, and potato tuberization daily Tmin condition. They are deterministic forecast indicators. The rice rule returns a condition flag; the wheat and potato rules also return condition flags. None of these verified evaluators returns a numerical risk index or severity category. The registry's generic hourly exposure-index formula and bands are not attached to these verified rules and do not establish a validated cross-crop or overall index. No score or color category is therefore justified for their current results.

The remaining five hazard families (`EXCESS_RAINFALL`, `COLD_FROST`, `DRY_SPELL`, `DISEASE_FAVOURABLE_WEATHER`, and `SEVERE_WEATHER`) have no verified executable rules. Crop recognition is separate from rule coverage: the `corn` alias resolves to canonical `maize`, which has no verified rule; spinach has one provisional heat candidate and no executable rule; carrot has no verified rule. No new rule was promoted in this review.

## Coverage expansion and rule-window index (2026-10-25)

No additional crop rule was made executable. Sources reviewed were promising associations or localized forewarnings, but did not define all required conditions in a way that can be evaluated from CropAI's current seven-day Open-Meteo feed and saved crop context.

- **Groundnut bud necrosis / thrips, Coimbatore:** the TNAU study used WRF extended-range forecasts for week 2 (days 8–14), summarized temperature, wind and RH as weekly averages and rainfall as a weekly sum. The thumb rule cites Tmax >33 C, RH 40–70%, wind >5 km/h and 0 mm rainfall. CropAI's current forecast is seven days and cannot evaluate this 8–14 day window or the source's WRF product; no rule is added. [Publisher article](https://arccjournals.com/journal/legume-research-an-international-journal/LR-5393); [official TNAU advisory reference](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/).
- **Groundnut leaf spot:** the IMD MAUSAM paper's field criteria include Tmax <=30 C, morning RH >=80%, afternoon RH >=60%, cloud amount 2–8 oktas with rainfall, at pod formation through maturity. The provider does not return morning/evening RH or cloud amount; no partial-condition rule is added. [IMD MAUSAM paper](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/1532).
- **Groundnut Alternaria leaf blight:** the field study in North Gujarat reports weekly combinations of Tmax/Tmin, maximum/minimum RH, rainfall, rainy-day count and sunshine, with crop-age dependence. Its paper describes exploratory regional relationships and ranges, not a transferable operational forecast classifier with a complete Boolean trigger; current fields also lack the saved planting-date matching and daily RH extrema/sunshine needed for the reported weekly model. No rule is added. [Study](https://www.mdpi.com/2036-7481/17/10/210).
- **Potato late blight:** PAU describes >90% RH, 10–20 C and cloudy/foggy conditions persisting 6–7 days in November–December, with main-season crop context. Cloud/fog and crop-season applicability are not represented by this forecast assessment, and the source page does not expose its full DS-value calculation. No disease rule is added. [PAU conditions](https://pau.edu/potato/favourable_cond.php); [PAU system description](https://pau.edu/potato/about_project.php).
- **Tomato early/late blight:** TNAU describes favorable or optimum conditions, but not an executable threshold-duration combination. In particular, "extended wet conditions," "cool nights," and "warm days" are not given numeric operational bounds. No rule is added. [TNAU late blight](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_8.html); [TNAU early blight](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_2.html).
- **Maize, cotton, soybean, chickpea, mustard, onion:** the evidence reviewed in prior notes remains incomplete for a provider-compatible forecast rule. Weather correlations, optimal growth ranges, lab or greenhouse experiments, and seasonal advisory statements alone are not converted into executable risk rules.

The rule-window weather exposure index is calculated independently for each verified deterministic rule as `round_half_up(100 * exposed observations / valid observations)`. The verified rice rule uses 48 complete hourly temperatures; the wheat rule uses its five consecutive daily Tmax values; the potato condition uses two consecutive future daily Tmin values. Values are bounded 0–100, with 0 meaning no samples matched and 100 meaning all samples matched. Intermediate values include the exact numerator/denominator and sample unit in the API. This index does not measure disease probability, yield loss, or agronomic severity. No severity bands are assigned, and no cross-hazard/crop-wide score is computed because the rules use different units and windows and have no common validated weights. The two-day potato horizon and 48-hour rice horizon remain application reporting windows, not source-established damaging durations.

No rule threshold, growth-stage scope, region, recommendation, or source verification status was changed in this expansion pass.
- **Mustard powdery mildew:** the Sardarkrushinagar study uses 12 years of weekly disease/weather observations and reports an equation involving Tmax and morning RH-I. The current hourly humidity stream does not supply the source's station-defined morning RH-I aggregation, and the published regression is a site-specific disease-severity model rather than a validated transferable forecast rule. No rule is added. [Study](https://journalajaar.com/index.php/AJAAR/article/view/492).
- **Chickpea collar rot:** the BCKV pathosystem model uses soil moisture and soil temperature for juvenile-stage disease incidence; neither soil variable is supplied by the current weather provider. No rule is added. [Journal of Agrometeorology paper](https://journal.agrimetassociation.org/index.php/jam/article/view/780).
- **Onion purple blotch:** the field study reports different weather-variable associations across seasons and years, not a transferable trigger with a fixed threshold-duration conjunction. No rule is added. [ICAR Indian Phytopathology paper](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/12707).
