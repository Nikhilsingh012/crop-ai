## Candidate 1 — Uganda cassava brown streak annual field surveys

**Official/publisher records:** [Scientific Data article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-019-0334-9); [Figshare dataset record](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.9273536.v1) (also identified by the article's machine-readable metadata as Figshare item 9273536).

**What is available:** The article says the public record contains DATASET_A.csv (126,779 plant rows), DATASET_B.csv (4,956 per-field records), DATASET_C.csv (7,627 unified per-field summaries), and LEGEND_CBSD_DATA.xlsx. It explicitly says the CSV files are readable in common analysis tools. Thus the file formats and filenames are publisher-verified; the deposit is a public Figshare item with no special institutional permission described. I could not retrieve the binaries from this network, so I did not independently verify file sizes, hashes, actual headers, or current download response.

**Observations and schema evidence:** These are real farmer-field cassava surveillance observations, not images or lab-only measurements. Dataset A holds plant-level symptoms; B/C are field summaries. The paper's field-variable table names date, time, year, latitude, longitude, village, crop_age_months, field_size_m2, variety_sampled, cbsd_foliar_presence, cbsd_foliar_severity, cbsd_foliar_incidence, and cbsd_foliar_severity_mean; exact column names can vary by dataset A/B and should be confirmed against the XLSX legend. Outcome is visual cassava brown streak symptom presence/severity; the paper does not distinguish the two causal virus species. Disease severity is on a 1–5 score where 1 denotes no visible symptoms. The 30-plant sampling frame per field supports incidence calculations. The exact survey date (rather than just year) and field coordinates are reported for Dataset A; paper says coordinates are expected accurate to within 100 m. Dataset B's legacy summaries have less uniform validation and should be treated separately or flagged.

**Weather matching:** Strongest candidate. Use Dataset A/C rows with verified dates and coordinates, extract weather for pre-observation exposure windows, and preserve survey time/season and crop age. Avoid interpreting an annual visit as daily repeated surveillance. The 2016 gap is explicit; 2012 has no retrievable Dataset A paper forms, although Dataset B includes summaries for 2012. The paper reports small presence/absence error rates in matched B records and notable whitefly-mean discrepancies; it also cautions that field symptom interpretation differences cannot be quantified. Do not treat the less-verified B rows as equivalent to A.

**Licence/access:** The paper states the Figshare data are freely available under CC BY 4.0. Cite the article and dataset DOI, and follow attribution terms. The [direct dataset page](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Cassava_brown_streak_disease_annual_field_survey_data_from_2004_to_2017_in_Uganda/9273536/1) is the route; choose Dataset A/C and the XLSX legend. The download is standard public Figshare download; no account or institutional authorization is specified. Current file-host response was not independently tested here.

## Candidate 2 — PlantVillage field attribute surveys, Kenya

**Official/publisher records:** [Peer-reviewed Frontiers article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2021.514785/full); [season 1 ScholarSphere DOI](https://doi.org/10.26207/3swt-8078); [season 2 field-attribute ScholarSphere DOI](https://doi.org/10.26207/k40m-1393).

**What is available:** The article describes CSV field-attribute data deposited in Penn State's public ScholarSphere. Season 1 is DOI 10.26207/3swt-8078; season 2 field attribute records are 10.26207/k40m-1393. Article states that the global community can access them. It says field-attribute data are under a Public Domain Mark, because the factual data are not copyrightable. The article distinguishes these field attributes from farmer names and field images, which are not included in the same dataset and have separate licensing. The paper describes CSV format, but the specific file names, exact header row, file size, and row count were not visible in the retrieved repository metadata; these need checking after opening/downloading the ScholarSphere items. No request for institutional permission is stated, but the article's public-access statement is the evidence, not a successful binary retrieval from this environment.

**Observation content:** Field data were collected by surveyors in Busia County, Kenya, with farmer consent. The ODK data-collection method records GPS field location/boundaries, crop type and mixture, survey date/time, planting/harvest dates, crop density, and disease prevalence for cassava mosaic virus, cassava brown streak virus, green mites, and maize fall armyworm; it also records comments and images separately. Data collection for season 1 is specified as 13 August–12 December 2019 and comprises 10,213 fields for field-composition work. The article does not establish that every field has disease prevalence measured, provide the actual field-level disease headers, quantify disease severity, or give date/location precision as encoded in the CSV. Disease fields should not be assumed populated simply because they are in the survey protocol.

**Weather matching:** Potentially useful if the exported CSV has populated disease prevalence, valid timestamp fields, and coordinates for those same records. GPS boundaries/points and survey dates are described in the protocol, but the actual data values must be checked. Survey design was not a probability sample; teams worked through local farmer networks and covered Busia longitudinally. Season-2 timing and differences in data collection/storage need separate verification. This is a candidate for county-scale association analysis, not automatically a representative district alert benchmark.

**Exact access route:** Open the two ScholarSphere DOI links above and use the repository's public item download action for the CSV field attributes. Do not use the separate image records as disease outcomes. Verify the item's Public Domain Mark and metadata on the item page before redistribution; follow the paper's distinction that images are separately licensed.

## Candidate 3 — Cassava Spectral and Image Dataset, Uganda

**Official/publisher records:** [Harvard Dataverse dataset page](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/R0KL7R); [peer-reviewed Data in Brief article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2023.109387); [dataset datasheet hosted by Makerere AI4D hub](https://makerere-university-ai4d-hub.emergentai.ug/dataset/8f11a7b9-362b-4f83-9c43-bbcd75767ca7/resource/279828ab-6ffd-41dd-b10b-0a9d40151b24/download/cassva_spectrometry_datasheet.pdf).

**What is available:** The dataset is deposited at Harvard Dataverse under DOI 10.7910/DVN/R0KL7R. The Data in Brief paper describes raw and analyzed data in screenhouse and open-field experiment folders; raw spectra are instrument CSV files, processed/concatenated records are CSV, and corresponding leaf images are JPEG. The datasheet says both raw and preprocessed data are provided. Its stated licence is CC BY (credit required); confirm the specific license and file list on the Dataverse item because the repository's per-dataset terms are authoritative. The paper describes the actual processed-label columns as class, week, variety, plant, leaf, image_label, image_name, expert_score, and chemistry test; there are 3,652 spectral features in addition to labels. These names are from the peer-reviewed article's printed schema, not a locally inspected CSV header.

**Observation content and distinction:** This is real crop disease status at plant/leaf level for cassava mosaic disease (CMD), cassava brown streak disease (CBSD), and healthy controls. It is mixed experimental data, including a controlled screenhouse subset and an open-field subset; it is not historical farmer-field surveillance. Plants were monitored weekly for 19 screenhouse weeks and 15 open-field weeks; “week” is a relative sequence index, not a calendar date. Label classes identify healthy/CMD/CBSD, and expert score plus lab chemistry provide related outcome measures. No GPS, district, field coordinate, or calendar date columns were verified. The institute association is not adequate to impute a field coordinate.

**Weather matching:** Not defensible as a retrospective weather-risk evaluation without supplemental experiment metadata specifying exact dates and open-field site coordinates. Screenhouse observations should not be treated as naturally weather-exposed field outcomes. The data are appropriate for disease-image/spectral diagnosis evaluation, not this project's alert validation in current form.

**Access route:** Open the Harvard Dataverse page and select its public file downloads. The article identifies the repository and persistent DOI; no special institutional permission is reported. The repository page itself was inaccessible through this execution network, so file-level names/sizes and live download success remain unconfirmed here.

It is a follow-up lead, not counted as verified among the three until the downloadable files and actual columns can be inspected.

## Recommended next action

Prioritize obtaining and auditing the Figshare Uganda dataset because it is the only candidate found here that combines a multi-year field surveillance series, exact survey dates, GPS locations, measured incidence/severity, substantial sample size, and stated CC BY 4.0 access. Use the public Figshare item link and download Dataset A, Dataset C, and the column legend; record file hashes and inspect actual headers, missingness, coordinate/date validity, duplicate field visits, and A/B provenance before any weather join. If the Figshare file host is unavailable from this environment, retrieve it from a normal network path to the same public item; do not substitute the paper's summaries for the records. Before using it in CropAI, assess whether a cassava CBSD alert is within project scope; it is geographically outside India and should be treated as an external methodological validation set rather than Indian performance evidence.
